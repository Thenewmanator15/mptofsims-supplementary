What this page shows

It answers one question: can the driver be set so that its two channels make identical pulses, at the pulse widths multiplexing needs?

The gate is driven by two channels in opposite directions, and the drive only cancels cleanly if both pulses are the same width. They are not: one comes out wider, by up to about 2.6 ns in these measurements. That difference is the mismatch. It gets smaller as the drive voltage rises, so a straight line through the measured points reaches zero at some voltage: the null, the setting at which the two channels would match.

Symbol widthNull, untunedNull, tunedReachable (≤ 80 V)?
  • Tuning moves the null to a lower voltage; it does not remove the mismatch.
  • Narrower pulses push the null higher. At 20 ns, the width multiplexing needs, no voltage the driver can deliver matches the channels. That is one reason the driver cannot multiplex cleanly (thesis figure BNG driver mismatch null).
  • A null above the highest voltage measured (about 79 V) is an extrapolation. Read the 20 ns figures as "well beyond reach", not as precise voltages.
