What this page shows

It asks: does every ion produce the same pulse, and if not, which ones differ and why?

Each ion that reaches the detector gives a pulse a few nanoseconds long. The recording holds 24,096 of them; the 23,759 that arrived on their own (nothing just before them) are shown here, each as 32 samples lined up on its peak. Heights are in units of the baseline noise (σ), because the converter's volts per code was not recorded.

GroupEventsMedian heightWhat they are
  • Almost every pulse has the same shape; they differ mainly in height, which is set by the detector's gain statistics.
  • Ions arrive in bursts far more often than chance allows: 161 runs of three or more within 5 µs, against about nineteen expected. The first pulse of a burst is unusually large and the ones after it are ordinary, which points to afterpulsing inside the detector rather than anything at the sample.
  • Despite the run's name, there is no copper here and the gate was not modulating: these are ions arriving at random, with no mass information.
