What this page shows

It asks: does the transformer pass the ion pulse intact, and what does it cost?

The detector gives negative pulses, and Appendix A reports a standing DC level on its output, but the converter wants positive pulses. The Coilcraft JA4220 deals with both: wired one way round it turns the pulse upside down, and like any transformer it passes no DC. The price is a little signal lost in the transformer and a small dip after each pulse, because it cannot pass the slowest part of the pulse.

QuantityValueMeaning
  • The pulse's energy sits well inside the band the transformer passes (panel 1), so the pulse comes through almost unchanged (panel 2).
  • The dip it adds is small: about a fifth of the dip seen on real captured ions, so the transformer is a contributor but not the main cause.
  • Wiring it the inverting way round costs nothing: it is slightly less lossy than the other way.
